# Characters — procedural people, the CHARAKTER editor and its 3D preview

Every player and passenger is a figure built from code (no models, no image files). A `Look` plus a few worn items define one person; the character tab in the menu edits them and shows a live 3D preview. The same data is what other players receive over the network, so a figure looks identical on every screen.

## How it works

- **Data.** `src/characters/look.ts:Look` holds the look (`female`, `build`, `skin`, `hair`, `hairCol`, `top`, `pants`, `shoes`, `hat`, `glasses`, `beard`, `face`, `topStyle`, `footwear`, `acc`, `short`). Option lists are `as const` arrays: `HAIRSTYLES`, `BUILDS`, `FACES`, `BEARDS`, `TOPSTYLES`, `FOOTWEAR`; colour palettes are `SKIN`, `HAIRCOL`, `TOPCOL`, `PANTCOL`, `SHOECOL`, `HATCOL`, `ACCCOL`. Colours are plain `0xrrggbb` numbers picked from those palettes. `src/characters/person.ts:PersonState` is what travels: `{ name, look, items }`, where `items` maps a `Slot` to an `ItemId`. IDs and numbers only, never geometry. *Why:* a few bytes per person keeps sync trivial and lets every client rebuild the same body.
- **Rolling.** `look.ts:makeLook` draws a random look; `src/game/players.ts:randomState` adds random items per slot using `src/characters/accessories.ts:SLOT_CHANCE`. `makeLook` draws the fields added later (face, beard style, top style, footwear, accessory colour) *after* the older ones. *Why:* a seed keeps the rest of its look from before those fields existed. Rarity is tuned in `src/config.ts:LOOKS` (`jokeFaceChance`, `beardChance`, plus hair/stubble material tuning); extreme builds are weighted rarer. Women never get a beard (the female body has no beard geometry).
- **Rig.** `src/characters/rig.ts:Rig` is the skeleton contract: root > torso > head, a `mouth` that opens with voice level, named `anchors` (`AnchorName`), `arms`, `fit` (body measurements for strap-like items), and the optional face `warp` and `hatBase`. Gameplay only talks to `Rig`. *Why:* a glTF body could replace the procedural one later by mapping its bones to the same names. `rig.ts:ARMPOSE` holds the arm angle presets (`hang`, `lap`, `hold`, `phone`, ...).
- **Body.** `src/characters/human.ts:rigHuman(root, look, pose, hat)` builds the whole figure for `BodyPose` `'sit'` or `'stand'`. Proportions per build are in its `BUILD` table (the `xxl` build deliberately spills over the seat).
- **Shells.** `src/characters/shell.ts:shell` and `surface` generate parametric patches of a deformed unit sphere (hair caps, beard bands, stubble); `table` is cosine interpolation through points; `taperedTube` builds tubes with a varying radius. *Why:* a hairstyle is a few outline functions instead of a pile of spheres, and stays one mesh and one draw call.
- **Hair and beards.** `src/characters/hair.ts:addHair`, `addBeard`, `hairMat` (procedural canvas textures, which are `null` in plain Node tests). `HAIR_TOP` is the hair's top height per style, which is where head items sit. `muetze` (beanie) is a "hairstyle" of its own and takes the `hat` colour.
- **Face shapes.** `src/characters/face.ts:faceWarp` is a smooth warp of head space; `warpMeshes` applies it to every mesh on the head (skull, jaw, hair, beard, eyes, hats) after they are built on the oval base head. `userData.noWarp` exempts parts (the neck). *Why:* hair and beard follow every face shape without per-shape variants, and the two head halves still close seamlessly because the warp keeps the mouth plane (`CUT_Y`) flat. The first four `FACES` are realistic; `gestreckt`, `laenglich`, `dorito` are the jokes.
- **Clothes.** `src/characters/clothes.ts:topMats` (materials per `TopStyle`; patterns are grey-scale masks tinted with the chosen top colour), `addCollar` (jackets only), `addFoot` (the four `FOOTWEAR` kinds).
- **Accessories and memes.** `src/characters/accessories.ts:ITEMS` is the registry: each `ItemDef` has a `slot`, a German `label`, a `build(ctx)` and optionally `tint` (`'hat'` or `'acc'`: which of the player's colours the item takes) and `underHat`. A new item is a new entry, nothing else. Most costume pieces live in `src/characters/memes.ts` (`ItemCtx` = look + body fit); `src/characters/itemkit.ts` has the cached materials/textures (`am`, `texOnce`, `pp`, `shade`). Content rule from the code comments: costumes and objects only, never faces or skin, no real brands, logos or clubs. `SLOTS` lists the offered slots; `hip` stays a type and anchor but has no items, so it is never offered, synced or rolled (old saves and peers that still send it lose it in `parseState`).
- **Person.** `person.ts:Person` owns the rig, equipped items and the smoothed head/torso state. `rebuild` after a look change disposes owned GPU buffers (`disposeOwned`, driven by `userData.owned`) and re-attaches items; `equip`, `unequip`, `toggle` manage items; `update(dt, t, yaw, pitch, rate, level)` advances the pose (head yaw comes from outside; torso follow-through, arm poses and mouth are computed locally and never sent; tuning in `config.ts:HEAD` and `config.ts:MOUTH`); `settle` snaps to the target pose; `state()` returns the `PersonState`. A hand item automatically pulls the arm into the holding pose.
- **Hats and hair.** A real hat (`ItemDef.underHat`) needs the hair styled to fit under it (curls flattened, crest and beanie off), so `Person.equip` calls `rebuild` whenever `underHat` flips; `fitHat` then bends the hat through the same face warp as the hair. *Why:* hats are modelled on the base head and must follow the face shape exactly like hair does.
- **Merge.** `src/characters/merge.ts:mergeStatic` fuses every group's direct mesh children that share material and role into one mesh (groups are the joints, so everything that moves still moves). *Why:* a figure drops from about 40 draw calls to about 15. Merged geometries are flagged `owned` so `disposeOwned` frees them; shared unit primitives (`src/characters/parts.ts:UCYL`, `USPH`, `UBOX`) and cached materials stay.
- **Parsing.** `src/net/protocol.ts:parseLook` and `parseState` validate anything from storage or a peer: unknown styles fall back to defaults, items must exist and sit in their own slot, `parseName` limits names (`NAME_MAX` = 16). The old boolean beard flag maps to `vollbart`. *Why:* saved profiles and older peers must keep loading as fields are added. `src/ui/store.ts:loadProfile` and `saveProfile` keep your profile in this browser.

## Using it

Menu, tab **Charakter** (side-by-side with the preview; the other tabs are a single column). `src/ui/menu.ts` creates `CharacterEditor` always and `CharacterPreview` only while the character tab is open. *Why:* the preview owns a WebGL context, freed by `dispose()` when the tab closes.

`src/ui/editor.ts:CharacterEditor(state, onChange)` works on a `structuredClone` and reports every change through `onChange`; the caller decides where it goes (preview, saved profile, network). Controls in order, German labels verbatim:

- **Name**: the placeholder shows the random fallback name (`setFallbackName`); hint "Steht über deinem Kopf. Leer lassen für einen Zufallsnamen."; `setName` takes over a name changed elsewhere without reporting back.
- Group **Aussehen**: Körper (Männlich/Weiblich), Statur (Dünn/Normal/Kräftig/XXL), Haut, Gesicht, Frisur, Bart (hidden for the female body), Haarfarbe (hidden for the beanie), Mütze/Hut (shown for the beanie or while an item tinted `hat` is worn), Oberteil, Schnitt, Hose, Schuhe, Schuhwerk, Extras (Brille, Kurze Ärmel).
- Group **Accessoires**: category tabs Outfits / Kopf / Hände / Körper / Boden (`CATEGORIES`; "Hände" covers the `hand` and `arm` slots, "Körper" covers `neck` and `body`), one row per slot with "Nichts" plus the items, and **Accessoire-Farbe** (shown while an item tinted `acc` is worn, or on the Outfits tab).
- **Outfits** (`accessories.ts:OUTFITS`, e.g. Alltag, Talahon, Genosse): one click sets `topStyle`, `footwear` and the item set; colours, face and body stay. *Why:* a preset is a costume, so the player's own choices survive.
- **Aussehen würfeln** (`makeLook(Math.random)`) and **Accessoires würfeln** (`randomState(...).items`).

`src/ui/preview.ts:CharacterPreview` is a small self-contained scene with its own renderer, so it works before the game world exists. Drag to turn it (it never spins on its own); at rest the figure idles by looking around a little. If WebGL is unavailable the renderer stays `null` and the canvas is simply empty. With a `floor` item (dog, crate) the camera steps back (`set`).
